## 3. THE LOCKED EMAIL FORMAT ("Meatier")

First-touch skeleton, **target 150–160 words** (multi-word brand names inflate raw counts; ~155–167 acceptable):
1. Warm opener: "Hope you are doing very well!"
2. Credibility line: "I spent ~19 years in analytics at Adobe, Walmart (Flipkart) and Sony, and now do returns-and-profit reads for DTC {category} brands — I took a quick look at {brand} from public signals."
3. **Blind-spot hook** — lead with what they CANNOT see in any single dashboard, NOT the known problem. Brand-specific, grounded in real research.
4. Concrete payoff: "With your data I can usually pin down {specific finding}."
5. Free/value line: "It's completely free — the kind of read a fractional CFO charges $2–5K for, at no cost while I pressure-test this on real data. If it's useful great; if not, you've lost fifteen minutes."
6. Soft binary close: "Would it be useful if I sent the short one-page read on {brand}?"

**$2–5K anchor** is web-grounded (a comparable one-off fractional-CFO analysis runs $5–10K, so $2–5K is conservative). The old "why free / I'm early" self-deprecating framing is **permanently retired**.

**Honesty rules (absolute):** no fabricated numbers / margins / cross-brand benchmarks for named prospects. Every claim measured against the brand's own history only.

**Draft discipline:** keep under ~162 words; wording changes mirrored to both Meatier and Lean; column M tracks type.

## 4. TOUCH-2 (SECOND TOUCH) — FORMAT + POLICY

**Mechanics (critical):** send as a **reply in the original thread ("Re: [same subject]")**, never a new email. Threaded follow-ups land better.

**Format ("Option B", chosen over a plain bump):** ~85–100 words, shorter than touch-1, opens "Following up on my note about {brand}'s returns," then leads with **ONE new concrete angle DIFFERENT from that brand's touch-1** (so it reads "still thinking about your business," not "repeating myself"), then a one-word ask ("reply 'yes'"). **No breakup line** ("last time I'll reach out" performs terribly — not yet, maybe never). Same honesty rules.
